    Updates at index maps, and whole loops, on UInt16Array #

    a.modifyAt idx lo hi g is a with g i a[idx i] written at the index idx i, for each i from lo up to hi - 1, in this order. It is the model of a loop that updates the elements at a map of indices: a stride a.modifyAt s.idx lo hi g, for a ScalarArray.Stride s, the diagonal of a matrix, a packed triangle or a permutation. When idx is injective on the range, which Stride.Fits.idx_inj gives for a stride, the element at idx i is g i a[idx i] (getElem_modifyAt_idx). The elements at no index of the range are unchanged (getElem_modifyAt_of_forall_ne). copySlice_eq_modifyAt gives a copy within bounds as a modifyAt at the indices destOff + i, so that a kernel can copy contiguous data with copySlice and strided data with a loop, and specify both the same way.

    The whole-loop lemmas of InPlace.Tail run a loop in continuation-passing style from any step j₀, where its one-step equation Tail.forInFrom_eq runs one step:

    • by Tail.run_forInFrom, a loop whose step j maps its variable and the array by f j runs as iterRange f, and by Tail.run_forInFrom_unit, a loop with no variable whose step j maps the array by F j runs as iterRange F. A loop of loops is of this kind, with the inner loops proved by the same lemmas;
    • by Tail.run_forInFrom_modify, the loop for j in lo...hi do a[e j] := g a[e j] of the inplace notation, where the value of the index e j is idx j, runs as modifyAt idx, and by Tail.run_forInFrom_write, so does for j in lo...hi do a[e j] := g;
    • the Tail.run_forInRevFrom lemmas do the same for the loops down, for j in reverse lo...hi, with iterRangeRev, and with modifyAt when idx is injective on the range.

    Two updates at a time have models too. a.modifyAt2 ia ib lo hi f g reads the elements at ia i and ib i and writes f i u v and g i u v back, for two index maps of one array, such as two rows of a matrix, and zipModifyAt a b ia ib lo hi f g does the same on two arrays. When the maps are injective on the range, and for modifyAt2 never meet, modifyAt2_eq and zipModifyAt_eq give them as modifyAts, whose lemmas then apply. Tail.run_forInFrom_modify2 runs the loop for j in lo...hi do let u := a[ea j]; let v := a[eb j]; a[ea j] := …; a[eb j] := … as modifyAt2.

    The strided loops modifyStride, modifyStride2 and zipModifyStride run these models on strides as single loops, which make each array exclusive once and then store without checks. They are specified by their models with @[csimp].

    The array a with g i a[idx i] written at the index idx i, for each i from lo up to hi - 1, in this order, where g i sees the earlier writes. Indices out of bounds are skipped. It is the model of a loop that updates the elements at the indices idx i, such as the steps s.idx i of a stride s.
